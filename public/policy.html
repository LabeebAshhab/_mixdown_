Usage policy

Last updated 16 November 2026

Mixdown is a tool, and what it is used for is up to the person using it. This page says what it may be used for, what limits this server sets, and what you can and cannot expect from it.

The short version

  • Download only what you have the right to: your own work, music under a licence that allows it, or music whose owner said yes.
  • What you fetch, and what you do with the files afterwards, is your responsibility.
  • Use it by hand, at a person's pace. The limits below are there so that everyone gets a turn.
  • Nothing is stored for you. A finished file waits %TTL_MINUTES% minutes at most and is then deleted.
  • Mixdown is free and comes as it is, with no guarantee that a link will work.

What Mixdown does

You give it a YouTube or SoundCloud link, or pick a song from a search. It fetches that audio, converts it to the format you chose, and adds tags, artwork and lyrics. Mixdown has no music of its own and no library: everything it handles comes from a public page that you pointed it to, is shown only to you, and is deleted within minutes.

What you may download

  • Music and recordings you made yourself or own the rights to.
  • Work in the public domain, or released under a licence that allows copying, such as Creative Commons.
  • Anything you have the owner's permission to download.
  • A copy for your own private use, where the law of your country allows one.

Do not use Mixdown to copy music you have no right to copy, to share, sell or upload the files somewhere else, or to get around a paywall, a sign-in or a regional block. Mixdown never signs in to any site, so private, members-only and age-restricted videos cannot be fetched; please do not try to make it.

YouTube and SoundCloud have terms of service of their own, and those terms may not allow downloading outside the options the sites themselves offer. Whether a particular download is allowed depends on those terms, on the licence of the song and on the law where you live. Checking that is up to you: Mixdown cannot check it for you, and nothing on this page is legal advice.

Whoever runs this server may slow down or refuse requests, block a visitor who ignores this page, change the limits, or switch a feature or the whole server off at any time.

What you get, and what you don't

  • The sound is as good as its source. YouTube and SoundCloud serve compressed audio. Converting it to FLAC, WAV, DSD or a high sample rate keeps that audio exactly as it is, in a much larger file, and cannot add detail that was never there. "Original" keeps the stream as it was delivered.
  • Tags, artwork and lyrics can be wrong. They come from public catalogues and are matched automatically. A match can be checked and corrected with the tag editor before you download.
  • Some videos cannot be fetched. Private and deleted videos in a playlist are left out, and the job says how many were skipped. A video that is age-restricted, blocked where the server is, a live stream or longer than %MAX_TRACK_MINUTES% minutes is marked as failed, and the rest of the playlist carries on.
  • A SoundCloud song may come from YouTube. When SoundCloud offers only a 30-second preview of a song, the whole song is looked for on YouTube instead, and the track says so. If it cannot be found, that track fails rather than handing you the preview as if it were the song.
  • It may stop working. The sites change often, and a link that worked yesterday can fail today until the server's tools are updated.

Artwork, lyrics, titles and the music itself belong to their owners. They are placed in your file for your own use of that file, and this gives you no further right to them.

The number of people online

The header shows how many people are using this copy of Mixdown at the moment. It is only a number: nobody can see who the others are or what they are downloading. The privacy page says how it is counted.

For copyright owners

Mixdown publishes nothing. It keeps no library, a file can be reached only by the visitor who asked for it, and every file is deleted when it has been downloaded or after %TTL_MINUTES% minutes. So there is nothing on this server to take down: the music stays on YouTube or SoundCloud, and a song removed or made private there can no longer be fetched here.

If you are concerned about how this particular server is being used, contact the person who runs it. Questions about the software itself are welcome through GitHub.
