Privacy & Policy

Last updated 17 November 2026

This page has two parts. Privacy says what Mixdown handles when you use it, where that goes, and when it is deleted. Usage policy says what Mixdown may be used for, what limits this server sets, and what you can and cannot expect from it.

Privacy Usage policy

Privacy

Mixdown is built to know as little about you as it can.

The short version

  • No account, no name, no email address. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers, and nothing loaded from other sites. Your browser talks only to this server.
  • One cookie: a random number that keeps your files yours. It is not used to follow you anywhere.
  • Your files are deleted from the server the moment you download them, or after %TTL_MINUTES% minutes if you don't.
  • A playlist larger than your space is fetched a part at a time. The songs still to come are only a list of titles and links in memory; none of them is stored until there is room.
  • The header shows how many people are online. It is a number only: nobody can see who the others are.
  • There is no database. Nothing about you is sold, shared or kept.

What the server handles

Your IP address

Like every website, the server sees the address your requests come from. Mixdown uses it for two things: to limit how many requests and links one visitor can have going, and to cap how much space all the browsers at one address may fill together (%IP_QUOTA_GB% GB). Your own space is counted for your browser, by the cookie described next, so other people at your address cannot use it up. The address is also noted with the check-in that counts the people online, described below. It is held in memory only. It is never written to disk or to Mixdown's log. The request counters are dropped after ten minutes without a request, and the copy attached to a download goes when that download is removed.

One cookie

The first time the app talks to the server it receives a cookie named sid, holding a random 64-character id. It lasts 30 days. It does two things: it decides which downloads your browser may see and fetch, and it is what your %QUOTA_GB% GB of space is counted for. If this server is protected by a password, the server also remembers, in memory and until it restarts, that your id entered it correctly. There are no other cookies.

Whoever held this cookie could fetch the files waiting for you, so it is kept out of reach: scripts cannot read it, it is never sent along with requests coming from other sites, and where this server is set up for https it is marked so that it never travels over an unencrypted connection. The id is a random number that cannot be guessed, and it is never written to the log.

The number of people online

The header shows how many people are using this copy of Mixdown right now. To count them, the server notes one thing each time the open page asks for its list of downloads, which it does every few seconds anyway: the time, kept under your random id together with your IP address. The page also says whether it is in view, so that a tab left in the background is not counted. A note is dropped %ONLINE_SECONDS% seconds after your browser last checked in; while a download of yours is in progress you are counted without one.

Only the total is ever sent to anyone. There is no list of visitors to read, no history of who was here or when, and nothing of it is written to disk or to the log. The IP address is there so that one machine cannot make the place look busier than it is. Whoever runs the server can switch the counter off, and then none of this is noted.

Links and search words

A link you paste is checked, reduced to the part that identifies the song or playlist, and handed to the download tool on the server; anything that is not a YouTube or SoundCloud link is refused. Words you type instead of a link are used as a search on YouTube, and only ever as search words. A link is held in memory as part of your download and disappears with it. When a playlist contains private or deleted videos, the server learns only how many there are, so that it can tell you they were skipped. Search words and their results are kept in memory, with nothing about who searched, so that a repeated search is quick; they are reused for ten minutes and dropped as newer searches come in or when the server restarts.

A playlist that is waiting for space

A playlist's list of songs, their titles and links, is held in memory like any other link. When the playlist is larger than your space, Mixdown fetches as many songs as fit and then waits for you to download them. During that wait the songs still to come exist only as that list: nothing of them is fetched from YouTube or SoundCloud, and nothing of them is stored, until there is room. This is why a long playlist never takes more than your %QUOTA_GB% GB on the server, however many songs it has.

If the part that is ready is not downloaded within %TTL_MINUTES% minutes, the playlist is cancelled: its files are deleted and the remaining songs are never fetched. What is left is the list of titles, so that the page can show you what happened; it is dropped %TTL_MINUTES% minutes later, or at once when you press the remove button.

The music

The audio is downloaded to the server, converted and tagged. What is kept sits in a folder with a random name: the finished file, a small MP3 for the in-page player (at most %PREVIEW_MINUTES% minutes long), the artwork, and a lyrics file if you asked for one.

  • A finished file is deleted as soon as it has been sent to you in full. The page then marks that song with a check mark.
  • Once everything in a download has been collected, the rest (the player's copy and the artwork) goes about 45 seconds later, or when you stop listening.
  • Anything you have not collected is deleted %TTL_MINUTES% minutes after the download finished. For a playlist that is waiting for space, those minutes run from the moment it stopped and start again each time you download one of your files.
  • Pressing the remove button on a download deletes all of it at once.
  • Whenever the server restarts, everything left on it is deleted.

Files you upload

Inspect a file. Only the kinds of audio file this server accepts are taken, and that is judged from the file's own bytes, never from its name. The file is saved under a random name and read once, for its tags and audio details, by a separate locked-down process that can open nothing else on the server. It is deleted straight away, whether or not it could be read. Its name never leaves your browser, and what comes back is shown to you as plain text.

Your own cover image. The picture is checked and re-encoded, and only the re-encoded copy is kept, together with the song it belongs to, until that song is deleted.

Tags you correct

A title and artist you type in the tag editor are used to look the song up again and are written into your file. They are kept with the download and go with it.

The server log

Mixdown writes short technical lines: the random id of a download, which catalogue matched, the file type and size, and, when something fails, the error text of the tool that failed, which can name the video or track involved. It does not write IP addresses, cookies, search words or the names of uploaded files.

The GitHub and website links in the footer lead away from this site. They open without telling the other site which page you came from.

What stays in your browser

Your preferences are kept in your browser's own storage for this site, on your device:

  • the theme (light or dark) and the language;
  • the format you last chose, its options, and the five switches under them;
  • the player's volume;
  • up to 18 small album covers of songs you fetched, used for the artwork drifting in the background.

The format, its options and the choices that shape the file (lyrics, loudness, album) are sent with each download you start, because the server needs them to make your file. The rest never leaves your browser.

Your browser may also keep a copy of the app's own files (the page, its styles and scripts) so that it opens quickly and when you are offline. Songs, artwork and everything else belonging to your downloads are never stored that way.

If you install Mixdown on a phone and share a link to it from another app, the link arrives in the page's address, is placed in the link field and is then removed from the address. Nothing starts until you press the button.

Your choices

  • Remove a download with its remove button, and its files are deleted from the server immediately. For a playlist this also drops the songs that were still to come.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser to remove the cookie, your preferences, the saved covers and the stored copy of the app.
  • You can block the cookie, but Mixdown cannot work without it: it is how the server knows which files are yours.

Usage policy

Mixdown is a tool, and what it is used for is up to the person using it.

The short version

  • Download only what you have the right to: your own work, music under a licence that allows it, or music whose owner said yes.
  • What you fetch, and what you do with the files afterwards, is your responsibility.
  • Use it by hand, at a person's pace. The limits below are there so that everyone gets a turn.
  • Nothing is stored for you. A finished file waits %TTL_MINUTES% minutes at most and is then deleted.
  • A playlist larger than your space comes in parts: download the part that is ready and the next one starts. A part left waiting for %TTL_MINUTES% minutes cancels the playlist.
  • Mixdown is free and comes as it is, with no guarantee that a link will work.

What Mixdown does

You give it a YouTube or SoundCloud link, or pick a song from a search. It fetches that audio, converts it to the format you chose, and adds tags, artwork and lyrics. Mixdown has no music of its own and no library: everything it handles comes from a public page that you pointed it to, is shown only to you, and is deleted within minutes.

What you may download

  • Music and recordings you made yourself or own the rights to.
  • Work in the public domain, or released under a licence that allows copying, such as Creative Commons.
  • Anything you have the owner's permission to download.
  • A copy for your own private use, where the law of your country allows one.

Do not use Mixdown to copy music you have no right to copy, to share, sell or upload the files somewhere else, or to get around a paywall, a sign-in or a regional block. Mixdown never signs in to any site, so private, members-only and age-restricted videos cannot be fetched; please do not try to make it.

YouTube and SoundCloud have terms of service of their own, and those terms may not allow downloading outside the options the sites themselves offer. Whether a particular download is allowed depends on those terms, on the licence of the song and on the law where you live. Checking that is up to you: Mixdown cannot check it for you, and nothing on this page is legal advice.

Playlists larger than your space

When a playlist does not fit in your %QUOTA_GB% GB, Mixdown fetches as many songs as fit and then stops. The songs still to come are listed together under "Waiting for space". They have not been fetched and take no room on the server.

  • Download the part that is ready. With "Download as soon as it's ready" switched on this happens by itself; otherwise press the "Download part" button. Your browser may ask once whether this site may download several files. If it blocks a download, the button is always there.
  • The playlist then carries on. As soon as a part has been sent to you in full it is deleted from the server, your space is free again, and the next songs are fetched. The playlist takes its turn in the queue like a new link, so it never holds up other visitors while it waits.
  • One folder. Each part is a zip holding a folder with the playlist's name, and the songs are numbered by their place in the playlist. Unpack every part into the same place and they join into one folder, in order. Mixdown cannot choose where your device unpacks a zip: some tools make a new folder for each zip unless you pick the same destination.
  • Check marks. A song you have downloaded gets a check mark, and the playlist shows how many of its songs you have. A downloaded song is gone from the server and cannot be downloaded a second time.
  • Do not leave a part waiting. If a ready part is not downloaded within %TTL_MINUTES% minutes, the whole playlist is cancelled: its waiting files are deleted and the remaining songs are not fetched. To get them, paste the link again.
  • A song that cannot fit. A single song larger than your whole space cannot be made here and is marked as failed; the rest of the playlist carries on.
  • A shared address. People who reach this server from the same network address share %IP_QUOTA_GB% GB between them. When that is full and you have nothing of your own waiting, a song fails with a message instead of waiting. The next section explains it.
  • Restarts. Nothing is kept across a restart of the server, so a playlist in progress ends there. Songs you already downloaded are yours; paste the link again for the rest.

Why "the space shared by your network address is full"

This is the message:

The space shared by everyone at your network address is full right now. Try again in a few minutes.

Nothing is wrong with your link, your browser or your device. Here is what happened.

  • Space is counted twice. Your browser has %QUOTA_GB% GB of its own for finished songs that are waiting to be downloaded. On top of that, all the browsers that reach this server from the same network address may have %IP_QUOTA_GB% GB waiting between them.
  • An address is often shared. Everyone on the same connection has the same address: the people in a home or an office on one Wi-Fi, a school or university network, and, with many mobile carriers, a large number of phones that have nothing to do with each other.
  • The shared space was full. Other people at your address had finished songs waiting that they had not downloaded yet, enough to use up the shared %IP_QUOTA_GB% GB. Your own space was empty, but there was no shared space left to put your song in.
  • Why the song fails instead of waiting. A playlist waits when you can make room yourself, by downloading the songs of yours that are ready. Here you had none. The waiting files belong to the other people, and you cannot see or download them. A playlist left waiting would have sat there with nothing you could do about it, so the song is marked as failed at once and you are told why.
  • Why there is a shared limit at all. A browser can throw its cookie away and come back looking like a new visitor with fresh space. Without a limit for the address, one person could fill the whole server that way and leave nothing for anyone else.

What to do. Try again a little later. The shared space is free again as soon as the other people download their songs. Songs that nobody downloads are deleted after %TTL_MINUTES% minutes; if someone at your address is working through a long playlist part by part, it can take longer. When there is room, paste the link again and it starts from the first song.

What the others can see. Nothing. The server only adds up how much space is in use at the address. Nobody at your address can see what you download, and you cannot see what they do.

What you get, and what you don't

  • The sound is as good as its source. YouTube and SoundCloud serve compressed audio. Converting it to FLAC, WAV, DSD or a high sample rate keeps that audio exactly as it is, in a much larger file, and cannot add detail that was never there. "Original" keeps the stream as it was delivered.
  • Tags, artwork and lyrics can be wrong. They come from public catalogues and are matched automatically. A match can be checked and corrected with the tag editor before you download.
  • Some videos cannot be fetched. Private and deleted videos in a playlist are left out, and the job says how many were skipped. A video that is age-restricted, blocked where the server is, a live stream or longer than %MAX_TRACK_MINUTES% minutes is marked as failed, and the rest of the playlist carries on.
  • A SoundCloud song may come from YouTube. When SoundCloud offers only a 30-second preview of a song, the whole song is looked for on YouTube instead, and the track says so. If it cannot be found, that track fails rather than handing you the preview as if it were the song.
  • It may stop working. The sites change often, and a link that worked yesterday can fail today until the server's tools are updated.

Artwork, lyrics, titles and the music itself belong to their owners. They are placed in your file for your own use of that file, and this gives you no further right to them.

The number of people online

The header shows how many people are using this copy of Mixdown at the moment. It is only a number: nobody can see who the others are or what they are downloading. The privacy part of this page says how it is counted.

For copyright owners

Mixdown publishes nothing. It keeps no library, a file can be reached only by the visitor who asked for it, and every file is deleted when it has been downloaded or after %TTL_MINUTES% minutes. So there is nothing on this server to take down: the music stays on YouTube or SoundCloud, and a song removed or made private there can no longer be fetched here.
